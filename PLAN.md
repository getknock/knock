## 1. Summary

A Wedgie (Pico + 240x240 LCD + Trust M secure chip) runs the **Knock app**: its own small Wedgie app, built from Safe Signer's parts, with its own key (D12, D17).

- **Setup, once per Wedgie**: bind the device key to a World ID 4.0 nullifier through a `KnockBinding` contract on World Chain (it emits `Bound`). A human proves themselves with World App once; their World ID session binds each Wedgie after, as the same human (D24). Then name the device in ENSv2 (for example `wedgie.itzraj.eth`) with `knock.*` records pointing at that binding (D21).
- **A knock**: a dApp opens the **Door** popup → the Wedgie shows the origin + KNOCK → green press → a signed `KnockPass`.
- **Verification**: the site's own verifier checks ENS (active, key), the `Bound` receipt on World Chain, the P-256 signature, the counter, and that the nullifier matches across device, ENS and log.
- **Kill switch**: from the Console, the user's wallet unregisters the name and unlinks its records, and the Wedgie forgets its name and binding (D20). It can be bound and named again after, as the same human (D24).
- Nobody sits in the middle, and Knock's server isn't involved at knock time.

| D25 | **Naming in one transaction, and the kill switch in one, when the wallet can** (the user's call: why several transactions?). ## 3. Design essentials

**Trust model**: one P-256 key, Knock's own: made on the device in Trust M slot `KEY4` (or `KEY3`), never Safe Signer's `KEY2` (D17). Its public key is in the app's save and in ENS (`knock.key`). Three independent authorities:
1. **Identity** = ENSv2 records on the device name.
2. **Humanity** = a `Bound` event on World Chain.
3. **Presence** = the device signs the site's fresh challenge after a physical green press.

Knock never touches funds.

**Wire (Wedgie ↔ Door)**: JSON lines over USB serial, ≤ 6 KB each, `id` echoed. Messages: `knock_hello` → `knock_info`; `knock_qr {n, bits}` → `knock_qr_shown {n, scale, logo}` (draws the computer's QR, no signing; D13); `knock_bind` → `knock_bind_sig` (press); `knock_enroll` → `knock_enrolled` (press; with the chip's vouch, `att`); `knock_confirm` → `knock_confirmed` (stores name, status, nullifier, PoH pointer; drives the badge); `knock_forget` → `knock_forgotten {name}` (forgets all of that: the kill switch's last step, D20); `knock_pass` → `knock_pass_sig` (press); `knock_cancel` → `knock_cancelled` (takes a QR down). Errors are `knock_error {error, why}`: the app sends `bad_request, rejected, timeout, not_enrolled, already_enrolled, no_key, too_large`; `busy, cancelled, internal` are reserved. The app keeps answering the firmware's `hello`/`ping` and adds `"knock": 1`, `knock_state` (`ok|none|lost|nochip`), `knock_key`, `knock_qr` (a QR is up) and, until there's a key, `knock_slots`. All of it passes on the chip (M1).

**Device rules**: states IDLE / PROMPT / QR / SIGNING. No signing without a physical green press made after the prompt is fully shown; presses in the first 0.6 s and USB-simulated presses are ignored. Red, 120 s of silence, or `knock_cancel` return to IDLE. The device computes every digest itself from fields it parsed. A monotonic counter is incremented and stored before every `knock_pass` reply. The device has no clock. Screens name the action in words (BECOME, BIND, KNOCK), the device name, and the origin labelled "unverified". Never show button letters.

**Signed structures** (EIP-712, domain `Knock` v`1`): `KnockHello`, `KnockEnroll`, `KnockBind`, `KnockPass(challenge, origin, deviceNode, counter, mode, nullifier, humanRef)`. Signatures are P-256 `r||s`, 64 bytes, low-s, over the digest as given (no re-hash).

**ENS records** on `<label>.<parent>`: `addr` (60), `knock.v=1`, `knock.key` (33-byte compressed P-256), `knock.status` (`active|revoked`), `knock.nullifier`, `knock.poh` (`eip155:480:tx:<hash>`), `knock.sc` (`S1|S2`), optional `knock.att`. The device name sits in a `UserRegistry` subregistry of the parent (made through the Verifiable Factory if the parent has none), with a Permissioned Resolver: the parent's own when the wallet can write it, else a new one. The wallet holds every role in both; the name gets the registrant's roles plus unregister and renew, and no expiry of its own. The device key holds no role.

**`KnockBinding`** (v3, D22, D24; ownerless, no upgrade path; `contracts/src/KnockBinding.sol`): two ways in, each with the device's yes and the human's. The device's: its P-256 signature over `KnockBind(block.chainid, this, nullifier, expiresAtMin, K)` (`bindDigest`, a view) via `P256VERIFY` at `0x100`, **s low** (the precompile takes either s); a zero `node` is refused (`NoName`). A human's first binding, `bind(nullifier, nonce, expiresAtMin, issuerSchemaId, genesisIssuedAtMin, proof[5], commitment, oprfSeed, x, y, node, r, s)`: a uniqueness proof that started a World ID session (`NoSession` without one: a zero commitment, or a seed not starting 0x01), checked by `WorldIDVerifier.verifyProofAndSignals(...)` with the deployment's action (`ACTION = keccak256(action) >> 8`; `action()` names it), its RP (`RP_ID`, fixed at deploy), `signalHash = keccak256(K ‖ node) >> 8`, where `K` is the 33-byte compressed key and `node` the name's namehash, and the session's commitment. It records the session (`sessions[commitment] = {nullifier, oprfSeed}`, `sessionOf[nullifier]` the human's latest) the first time it's seen, and emits `Human`. Every binding after it, `bindAgain(commitment, sessionNullifier[2], nonce, expiresAtMin, issuerSchemaId, genesisIssuedAtMin, proof[5], x, y, node, r, s)`: a session proof for a recorded session (`NotASession` otherwise, or for a nullifier whose action doesn't start 0x02), checked by `verifySession(...)`, for that session's human. Both record the human as the key's and the name's latest (`humanOfKey[keyHash]`, `humanOfName[node]`) and emit `Bound(nullifier, keyHash, node, expiresAtMin)` with `keyHash = keccak256(x ‖ y)`; World ID's no reverts. `VERSION` is 3. Anyone may submit either.

**World ID request**: IDKit v4, signal = `0x` + the 66 hex digits of the device's compressed key + the 64 of the name's node (D22). A human's first binding: preset `proofOfHuman`, the deployment's action, `session_id: "create"`. Every one after: `IDKit.proveSession(session)` with a `proof_of_human` constraint and the same signal (D24). `knockd` signs the RP context: with the action for a first binding, without one for a session proof. The Console shows `connectorURI` on the Wedgie via `knock_qr`.

**Door protocol** (`door-sdk`'s `knock()` and the Door's `/knock`): the dApp opens `<door-origin>/knock` as a popup, from a click; the popup posts `knock/ready` (to any origin: it says nothing else); the dApp posts `knock/request {challenge, mode}` to the Door's origin only. The Door listens to the window that opened it and nothing else (`event.source`), and takes the origin from `event.origin`, never from the message. It runs KNOCK on the Wedgie, checks the `KnockPass` that comes back, then posts `knock/result` to that origin only, once: `{ok: true, assertion}` or `{ok: false, error, why}`. It won't run framed. The assertion is `{v: 1, name, challenge, origin, deviceNode, counter, mode, nullifier, humanRef, r, s}`. Modes: **1 Bound** (humanity from the binding) and **2 Fresh** (a World ID proof per knock, `humanRef = keccak256(IDKit result)`; not built yet).

**Verifier (V1–V8, in order, fail closed)**:
1. Parse (low-s).
2. The challenge is ours, unexpired (≤ 2 min), unconsumed, and the origin is ours.
3. Resolve the name and check validity: not expired, `knock.v`, `status=active` seen ≥ 3 blocks deep, `revoked` honoured from the latest block, cache ≤ 60 s; a key seen revoked is invalid forever.
4. Recompute the `KnockPass` digest from our own challenge and origin; verify the signature under `knock.key`.
5. The counter is above the highest stored for this key.
6. Mode 1: `humanRef` is zero; the signed nullifier equals `knock.nullifier`; the receipt named by `knock.poh` succeeded on an accepted `KnockBinding` (v3, production), with a `Bound` log whose `keyHash`, `nullifier` and `node` (the name's namehash, D22) match, not expired, and final for a strict site (D26).
7. Mode 2: forward the IDKit result bytes to World's verify API and check `humanRef`, the action, and `signal_hash(challenge)`.
8. Optionally enforce one entry per (site action, nullifier).

Then commit atomically: consume the challenge, store the counter, insert the nullifier. A rejected knock consumes nothing. The answer is `{ok: true, knock: {name, key, nullifier, counter, origin, binding: {tx, block, provedAt}}}` or `{ok: false, check, error, why}` (400 for check 1, 503 when a chain couldn't be read, 403 otherwise). D23 says what "not expired" and the cache mean, D26 when "final" applies.

**Revocation**: the kill switch (D20) takes the name away: unregistered, its records unlinked, the Wedgie told to forget. A lighter revoke keeps the name: the user's wallet sets `knock.status = revoked` (one mainnet tx), and a key seen revoked never returns. Binding a new device does not cut off the old one; only the kill switch or a revoke does.

It's signed EIP-191 (`"\x19Ethereum Signed Message:\n81"`) with secp256k1, as `r‖s‖v`, v = 27 or 28. `hash_to_field(x) = keccak256(x) >> 8`. IDKit takes it as `rp_context {rp_id, nonce, created_at, expires_at, signature}`; the result's top-level `nonce` is the one the proof binds (`verify`'s `nonce`). Checked on chain (2026-11-06): the RP key file's key is the signer `getOprfKeyIdAndSigner` gives for Knock's RP in both World RP registries.
- **World App's replay guard** (read in WalletKit's source, its nullifier cache): before World App makes a uniqueness proof, it works out the nullifier and refuses one it has disclosed before (`NullifierReplay`), unless that was within 10 minutes (a retry); it remembers each for a year. A nullifier is the human's, the RP's and the action's, whatever the signal, and only RPs on World App's own list are let through. The user's World ID proved `knock-bind` twice in M3, so World App now refuses it; the Simulator's identity is another World ID, so it kept working (R15).
- **World ID sessions** (IDKit 4.6.0 and World's verifier contracts, read in their source): a session id is `session_` and 64 bytes in hex, the session's commitment and its oprf seed (first byte 0x01); World App derives the session again from the seed. A uniqueness proof made with `session_id: "create"` carries the commitment as a public input, so it's checked with `verifyProofAndSignals` and that commitment. A session proof is checked with `verifySession(rpId, nonce, signalHash, expiresAtMin, issuerSchemaId, genesis, sessionId, [nullifier, action], proof)`; its action is random with first byte 0x02, so World App's replay guard never applies. Both of World's deployed verifiers answer both functions (a zero proof reverts `InvalidMerkleRoot` in each, checked on a World Chain fork). World's developer portal answers a request for an action it doesn't have with a stand-in, so an action needn't exist there.

## 7. Risks

- **R1 (resolved 2026-11-06) – ENSv2 live status.** Verified on chain: ENSv2 is live and `itzraj.eth` is in the v2 registry (facts in §4). Both Universal Resolver addresses answer correctly; we use the proxy (D14). The verifier's chain config still supports Sepolia as a fallback.
- **R2 (corrected twice) – Expiry units.** World's `expires_at_min` / `expiresAtMin` is in **Unix seconds**, and "min" means minimum, not minutes (World's own example is `1756166400`). It's a lower bound on the credential's expiry, and World App sets it to about when it makes the proof, so in a `Bound` it says when the human proved, not until when. Verifiers bound a binding's age instead (D23); the tests cover a binding a year and a day old.
- **R3 (eased by D12) – Built from Safe Signer's parts.** Knock copies a few pieces (the line reader, the screens and their press rules, low-s signing) from upstream `safe-63` instead of tracking a fork, so there's nothing to merge. Fixes upstream, like its press-rule audits, are worth re-reading now and then.
- **R4 – World production vs staging.** Real World App users need a production action. Develop against staging + Simulator. Never accept staging deployments in a production verifier. The IDKit `environment`, the action's environment and Simulator-or-real-app must all match: a staging action with the real World App silently produces no proof (World's SKILL.md). Production is ready (§8); a staging run needs its own `knock-bind` action first.
- **R5 – 36 hours.** The core loop (M1–M5) comes first; see the cut order.
- **R6 – Gas key balance is ~$2.** Fine for binds: one costs ~0.00000063 ETH (§4), so the ~0.001 ETH left covers ~1,500. Watch it anyway. `knockd` sends only bindings that pass a simulation (D19), at most 12 an hour, which bounds the damage if it leaks.
- **R7 – Honest limits (keep in the README)**: no PIN on the Wedgie (a thief can knock until revoked); proof farming is not solved; the shared public nullifier is linkable across sites in Bound mode; installed apps can use the chip (S1 vs S2); the origin is "unverified" on the device; the Wedgie is not an audited hardware wallet.
- **R8 – Door origin.** Web Serial permission is per origin, and the port is exclusive (the Door and `wedgie.dev/safe` can't run together).
- **R9 – One wallet for controller and revoker** (D9) means a compromised wallet can both rename and revoke, and there's no separate low-power revoke key. Acceptable for now; revisit with a status-only role if time allows.
- **R10 (eased by D12) – Wedgie RAM.** ~74 KB was free at idle under Safe Signer. Knock is much smaller (~29 KB of source across four files), and its request code loads only for a request (`knock_knock`, `knock_key`). `tools/mpy.sh` builds `.mpy` files if RAM gets tight, and the QR is encoded on the computer (D13). Measured on the device: hello's `ram` is ~121 KB free at idle with Knock (versions 2 to 4 alike), against ~73 KB under Safe Signer. Measure again after each new message.
- **R11 (resolved by D17) – A shared Safe key.** Signing Knock messages with Safe Signer's `KEY2` key would have put the user's Safe signer key on chain and in ENS next to `itzraj.eth`, linking the Safe to the name in public, and a bug in a Knock screen could have signed Safe data. Knock has its own key instead, and never names `KEY2`.
- **R13 (resolved by D22) – A binding for any name.** In v1 a binding named no ENS name, and `knock_confirm` takes any name with no press. So whoever held a lost Wedgie could point a name of their own at its binding (the nullifier and the binding's transaction are public) and knock as its human on humans-only sites; the kill switch removed only the owner's name. A side agent found it in review. What's left: whoever controls the name's parent could re-point the name, and needs the Wedgie too; a parent that lapses can be someone else's.
- **R12 (resolved 2026-11-06) – An empty slot's answer.** trustm.md doesn't say which error code a signature gets from an empty slot, and Knock's first guess (0x01/0x03/0x05) was wrong. On the user's Wedgie both empty slots answered 0x0b ("command out of sequence"), so Knock made nothing and showed `error 0x0b` in `knock_slots`. Infineon's error table describes 0x0b as a command needing a private key the slot doesn't have. The user chose 0x0b as the only "empty" answer: any other answer, the old guesses included, leaves a slot alone.
- **R14 – The verifier's public RPCs.** A knock costs 10 `eth_call`s on mainnet (5 records at two blocks; the deep read is cached ≤ 60 s) and 2 calls on World Chain, on public RPCs by default. A rate-limited or failing RPC fails closed (`unavailable`, 503, nothing used up), and a site can set its own (`VERIFIER_ETH_RPC`, `VERIFIER_WORLD_RPC`).
- **R15 (resolved by D24) – World App proves a human for an action once.** Its replay guard refuses a nullifier it has disclosed, after a 10-minute retry window, for a year (§4). v2 asked for the human's `knock-bind` nullifier at every binding, so each human could bind once: no second Wedgie, and no binding again after a kill. World App said only that the request wasn't accepted, and the Simulator, another World ID, hid it. v3 asks for a uniqueness proof once per human and deployment, and for session proofs after. The user's World ID can't prove `knock-bind` again for a year, and a new deployment's action is a new first binding for every human.
- **R16 – A session's seed isn't in its proof.** A first binding's proof covers the session's commitment, not its oprf seed, so whoever sends a copy of the proof first, with another seed, would record that one, and `/human` would hand out a session World App can't prove. The proof still binds only its own device key and name (the signal, and the device's signature over the nullifier), `KnockBinding` keeps the first seed recorded, World Chain's sequencer keeps no public mempool, and the Console that made the binding remembers the seed IDKit gave it.
